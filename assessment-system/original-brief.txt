# PROJECT: BRAINOVA – INTELLIGENT ASSESSMENT & PLACEMENT SYSTEM

## 1. YOUR ROLE

Act as a Senior Educational Assessment Specialist, Psychometrician, Adaptive Testing Designer, Learning Analytics Specialist and Education Technology Architect.

Your task is to design a comprehensive, intelligent and reusable assessment platform for Brainova.

This platform must be capable of conducting diagnostic assessments, admission assessments, placement tests, academic assessments and topic-specific proficiency tests.

It must assess not only what a student scores but also what the student understands, how quickly they respond, which concepts they have mastered and which areas require further learning.

The ultimate objective is to recommend the most suitable learning level, course or academic starting point for each student.

This is not an ordinary quiz application. It is an intelligent student assessment and placement system.

#### Live Quiz Experience

Questions should appear on a large, clear presentation screen.

All participating students should receive the same question simultaneously.

Students answer individually using their own devices.

The teacher should have control over when the next question appears.

The interface should display:

- Current question number.
- Question content.
- Visuals or diagrams, where applicable.
- Answer options or response area.
- Countdown timer.
- Assessment progress.

The teacher should also have the option to enable automatic progression after a specified time.

Support different question formats, including MCQs, short answers, numerical answers and other appropriate formats.

### MODE B: INDEPENDENT TIMED ASSESSMENT

This mode is designed for students taking assessments independently.

The student receives access to an assessment through their account or a secure invitation.

The experience should include:

- Assessment instructions.
- Time limit.
- Start confirmation.
- Countdown timer.
- Question navigation, if permitted.
- Progress indicator.
- Automatic submission when time expires.
- Submission confirmation.

The teacher or administrator should decide whether students can:

- Review previous questions.
- Change their answers.
- Skip questions.
- Return to unanswered questions.

The system should record the time spent on every question.

### Parent Supervision

For young learners, provide an option requiring parent supervision.

Before the assessment begins, display a parent supervision confirmation.

Ask the parent to ensure that:

- The student works independently.
- No external assistance is provided.
- ChatGPT or other AI answer-generation tools are not used.
- The student does not receive help from another person.
- The assessment environment is appropriate.

Allow the parent to confirm supervision.

The platform must be honest about its limitations: a browser-based application cannot guarantee that students will not access other devices or external applications. Provide reasonable safeguards without claiming that cheating is impossible.

Where technically supported, offer full-screen assessment mode, randomized question variants, question timing, shuffled answer options and visibility of assessment activity.

Do not use invasive surveillance, facial recognition or unnecessary collection of personal information.

## 5. TWO QUESTION SELECTION AND DIFFICULTY MODELS

This is a critical feature.

The system must offer two different assessment models.

### MODEL 1: FIXED-DIFFICULTY ASSESSMENT

The teacher defines the number of questions from each difficulty category.

Use three standard categories:

**STRENGTHEN**
- Foundation knowledge.
- Basic concepts.
- Simple questions.
- Guided application.
- Fundamental skills.

**HARD**
- Intermediate and challenging questions.
- Multi-step reasoning.
- Conceptual understanding.
- Application-based problems.

**EXTEND**
- Advanced questions.
- Higher-order thinking.
- Complex problem-solving.
- Unfamiliar applications.
- Advanced conceptual understanding.

The teacher should decide how many questions are included from each category.

Example:

Total: 20 questions.

- Strengthen: 6 questions.
- Hard: 8 questions.
- Extend: 6 questions.

The system must respect the selected distribution.

The teacher should also be able to adjust the distribution and the total number of questions.

### MODEL 2: ADAPTIVE ASSESSMENT

Design an intelligent assessment inspired by adaptive testing approaches used in standardised examinations such as the GRE.

The difficulty of the next question should depend on the student's previous performance.

The system should operate as follows:

1. Start with an appropriate baseline question based on the selected grade, age and topic.
2. If the student answers correctly, consider a more challenging question.
3. If the student repeatedly answers correctly at higher difficulty levels, increase the difficulty progressively.
4. If the student answers incorrectly, consider a question at a lower difficulty level or test the prerequisite concept.
5. If performance fluctuates, continue assessing at an appropriate level rather than making extreme difficulty changes.
6. Identify the student's estimated proficiency for each topic.
7. Stop when sufficient evidence is collected or the configured assessment limit is reached.

Do not simply increase difficulty after every correct answer or decrease it after every incorrect answer.

Use the student's overall response pattern, accuracy, question difficulty and topic performance.

The adaptive system should assess the student's actual ability rather than merely their speed.

Response time can provide supporting information but must not independently determine whether a student is capable of answering difficult questions.

The system must be transparent about the adaptive logic and provide a teacher-reviewable explanation of its recommendations.

## 8. STUDENT PROFICIENCY AND PLACEMENT RECOMMENDATION

This is the primary purpose of the platform.

After completing the assessment, generate a comprehensive diagnostic report.

The report should recommend an appropriate learning level.

Possible recommendations include:

- Foundation / Beginner.
- Developing.
- Intermediate.
- Proficient.
- Advanced.

The teacher should be able to customise the proficiency categories.

For example, a student applying for an Addition course might receive:

**Assessment Findings**

- Basic addition: Strong.
- Addition with carrying: Proficient.
- Multi-digit addition: Developing.
- Word problems: Needs support.
- Mental mathematics: Advanced.

**Recommendation**

The student does not need to start with basic addition. Recommend a course focusing on multi-digit addition, carrying and mathematical word problems.

For a full Mathematics assessment, the recommendation should be topic-specific.

For example:

- Addition: Advanced.
- Subtraction: Proficient.
- Multiplication: Developing.
- Division: Foundation.
- Fractions: Needs assessment.

Do not force the student into one overall level when their proficiency differs across topics.

The system should recommend:

1. Most suitable course or level.
2. Topics the student is ready to learn.
3. Topics requiring revision.
4. Missing prerequisite concepts.
5. Recommended learning sequence.
6. Suggested additional assessments, if necessary.

Provide an explanation for every major recommendation.

Assessment results should support teacher judgement rather than automatically making irreversible admission decisions.

## 18. IMPORTANT ACADEMIC AND TECHNICAL PRINCIPLES

Follow these principles throughout development:

- Do not treat speed as the only measure of intelligence or ability.
- Do not use the overall percentage as the only basis for placement.
- Assess every selected topic independently.
- Use age-appropriate questions.
- Ensure difficulty levels are academically meaningful.
- Avoid repeated or overly similar questions.
- Ensure answer keys are accurate.
- Use sufficient questions to support reliable recommendations.
- Distinguish between unanswered, skipped and incorrect questions.
- Make adaptive decisions based on performance evidence.
- Keep the assessment fair and transparent.
- Preserve assessment history.
- Protect student data.
- Allow teacher review and intervention.
- Keep all student reports connected to their original assessments.

For adaptive testing, explain that the result is an estimated proficiency based on the questions administered. Avoid presenting a small diagnostic test as a definitive measure of a student's entire academic ability.
